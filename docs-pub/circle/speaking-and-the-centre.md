# Speaking and the centre

Only one person holds the centre at a time. Everything about speaking is really
about who holds it and how they got there.

## Taking the centre

**Press the space bar**, or click the control in the middle of the screen. What
it does depends on where you already are:

| Your situation | What the control does |
|---|---|
| Nobody is speaking, you are not queued | Takes the centre |
| Someone else is speaking, you are not queued | Puts you in the queue |
| You are queued but not speaking | Takes you back out of the queue |
| You are the speaker | Ends your turn |

If people are waiting when you end your turn, the next person in the queue takes
the centre automatically.

During a round the space bar means something slightly narrower — see
[Rounds](rounds.md).

## The queue

The queue decides who speaks next. The person at the front is the current
speaker; everyone else waits in order. An empty queue means nobody is speaking
and the centre shows the scene.

## Your microphone follows the centre

You do not unmute to speak. Taking the centre unmutes you, and losing it mutes
you again. See [Sound and camera](sound-and-camera.md).

## The three layouts

Your host picks one of three layouts per room. They change where the speaker
appears, not how speaking works.

### Centre

The speaker moves to the middle for everyone, including themselves. Their place
in the outer ring is empty while they are there.

### Centre with talking stick

Everyone else sees the speaker in the middle. **The speaker does not see
themselves there** — they see the centre media or the talking-stick image
instead, so they are looking at the room rather than at their own face. Their
place in the ring is still empty for everyone else.

### In place

The speaker stays where they are in the ring and their circle grows. The centre
does not show them at all, and keeps showing the scene's centre media if there
is any.
