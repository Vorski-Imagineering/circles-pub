# What you are looking at

A circle is a ring of people around a centre, over a background your host chose.

## The ring

Everyone in the circle, including you, appears as a circle around the edge. Your
own circle is one of them — you see yourself the same way everyone else sees you.

Positions are worked out from how many people are present, so the ring rearranges
itself as people arrive and leave.

## The centre

The middle of the screen is the speaking position. Whoever currently has the
centre appears there, larger than everyone else. When nobody is speaking, the
centre shows whatever the scene puts there — an image, a video, or nothing.

How the speaker appears depends on the layout your host chose. See
[Speaking and the centre](speaking-and-the-centre.md).

## The scene

Behind everything is a **scene** — a background image or video, and optionally
something in the centre. Your host picks it for the room; you cannot change it.

Scenes are set up by site admins, and a host may also override the background or
centre for one particular room.

## When people appear

**A person appears when their video does.** Not when they connect, not when they
are listed as present — when there is an actual picture of them.

This means a circle can be quieter than the participant count suggests for a
second or two while someone's camera starts up. That is deliberate: an empty
placeholder standing in for a face tells you nothing, so nothing is drawn until
there is something real to draw.

Practically:

- Someone who has just joined may not be visible yet, briefly.
- Someone whose video has frozen keeps their **last picture** on screen rather
  than vanishing, with a spinner over it, while the connection is repaired.
- Someone whose camera has gone away entirely will be taken out of the circle —
  see [Sound and camera](sound-and-camera.md).

## Muted people

Someone who is muted is shown with the muted treatment on their circle. Almost
everyone is muted almost all of the time; see
[Sound and camera](sound-and-camera.md) for why.

## The menu

The menu opens the actions available to you: getting back to
**Settings**, reporting a bug, and — if you are the host — the hosting controls.
See [Hosting basics](../host/hosting-basics.md) for those.
