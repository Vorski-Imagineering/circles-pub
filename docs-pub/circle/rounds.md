# Rounds

A round is a structured turn for everyone, instead of the open queue of normal
conversation. Your host starts it; see
[Running a round](../host/running-a-round.md) for that side.

## What changes when a round starts

Everyone in the room is put into one ordered queue, oldest joiner first, and the
first person takes the centre. You do not opt in — a round includes everybody
present when it starts.

## Your turn

When you reach the front, you take the centre and your microphone opens, exactly
as in normal conversation.

**Ending your turn:** click the speaking control or press the space bar. During
a round only the current speaker can do this — the space bar does not put you in
or out of a queue the way it does normally.

If the round has a time limit, you see a circular countdown around your circle
while you speak. Otherwise your turn runs until you end it.

When your turn ends you leave the queue and **do not rejoin automatically**. A
round gives everyone one turn.

## While you are waiting

You see your position in the queue. Clicking it leaves the queue, if you would
rather not take a turn.

## After you have spoken

Your control settles into a quiet "have spoken" state. There is nothing more to
do; the round continues without you.

## How a round ends

When the last person has spoken, the round ends by itself and the room returns to
normal conversation. A host can also stop it early, which returns everyone to
normal immediately.

If the person speaking drops out, the round moves on to the next person rather
than stalling.
